# Six-player 10-BB NLHE smoke and launch calibration

Date: 2026-07-14

## Outcome

The selected first poker game is six-player, full-deck NLHE with 10-BB stacks,
0.5/1 blinds, and a 0.5-BB fixed denomination.  It uses the existing poker
engine, range model, pre/postflop networks, sibling evaluator, completion path,
and branched Rao--Blackwell objective.

The launch preset is `configs/train/nlhe6_10bb.yaml`.  It deliberately keeps
the calibrated 96-wide model and FP32 execution rather than increasing model
capacity at the same time as beginning the first long poker run.

## Completed hour smoke and launch gate

The subsequent two-rank `nlhe6_10bb_gpu_smoke_hour` run completed all 1,200
iterations in 18 minutes 24 seconds.  It was operationally clean, but its
preflop intensity of 1.0 under-sampled public topology: across 2,400 rank-local
trees, mean/median/99th-percentile/maximum sizes were 9.63/8/28/126 nodes, and
2,397 trees contained fewer than 32 distinct decision rows.  Each decision row
still covers the complete private-hand range; the concern is repeated sampling
of a very small number of public decisions, not a 32-combo poker minibatch.

The run emitted no non-finite metrics or invalid posterior mass, and minimum
completion effective sample count was 62.70 out of 64.  Its first-iteration
measured allocator peak was 0.48 GiB; later iterations were not phase-profiled,
but live observation stayed far below the analytical 1.73-GB structural plan
and the approximately 8.6-GiB configured limit.  Entropy fell from 0.66 over
the first 100 iterations to 0.20 over the last 100.  In the final 100 updates,
the weighted value and policy terms were approximately 0.177 and 0.022, while
value explained variance remained near zero.  This ruled out launching the
original long preset unchanged.

A 600-iteration `nlhe6_10bb_gpu_gate` then tested a combined launch candidate:
preflop beta 1.10, `c_v=0.0005`, and a 50-iteration KL reference interval.  It
completed in 10 minutes 44 seconds with zero invalid completion mass and no
non-finite metrics.  Against the first 600 iterations of the hour smoke:

- mean tree size increased from 8.18 to 17.03 nodes;
- mean decision count increased from 7.03 to 13.72;
- median/90th/95th/99th/maximum tree sizes were 10/22/30/180/681;
- final-100 entropy improved from 0.40 to 0.61;
- maximum weighted reference KL decreased from 0.83 to 0.48; and
- final-100 weighted value/policy terms became approximately 0.056/0.085.

The comparison changes three parameters and uses a different seed, so it does
not attribute the improvement to any one parameter.  It is an operational
selection of the combined candidate, not a hyperparameter-optimality claim.
The 681-node maximum is 16.6% of the 4,096-node cap.  Live device memory use was
about 1.6 GiB per rank during the gate.

The launch preset now has a 20,000-iteration ceiling and is intended to run
under a seven-hour external wall-clock timeout with checkpoints every 250
iterations.  Runtime is policy-dependent; the ceiling should not be read as a
promise that all iterations will finish within seven hours.

## Long-run legal-policy underflow and recovery

The first long-run attempt stopped during rank-one rollout after iteration 387
with the CUDA assertion `supplied legal events have zero policy mass`.  The
policy adapter previously softmaxed all three action types before removing
illegal types.  If an illegal type dominated the legal logits by roughly 100 or
more, fp32 softmax could underflow every legal type to exactly zero.  This was a
numerical conditioning failure; checkpoint parameters, completion metrics, and
the GPUs themselves were finite and healthy.

The adapter now preserves its ordinary probability path exactly, but falls
back to a legal-event logit softmax when the ordinary legal normalizer is zero.
An analogous conditional fallback handles an entire legal raise tail that
underflows to zero.  CPU and CUDA regressions cover an illegal raise type that
dominates check/fold/call by 2,000 logits.  The full suite passed with 204 tests
and 10 skips.

The 388-record pre-crash metrics were archived as
`metrics_precrash_0000388.jsonl`.  The run was rewound to checkpoint 250 and
replayed through the former failure point, including several 500--900-node
trees.  It reached checkpoint 500 without recurrence.  The active metrics file
was then trimmed to the exact 0--499 prefix required to resume checkpoint 500;
the four post-checkpoint verification records are separately preserved as
`metrics_verification_0000504.jsonl`.

## Correctness fix found by calibration

A branched frontier may contain player rows and chance rows at the same depth.
Generic event collation pads the chance-only card payload across that combined
batch.  Poker previously dispatched the whole batch as chance whenever the
payload contained `cards`, which raised `card payload requires chance states`.

`PokerRolloutBackend.step` now partitions rows by stored phase, applies player
and chance transitions independently, and scatters successors back into
original order.  A regression combines a legal preflop fold and a flop deal in
one batch.  The previously failing end-to-end branched calibration then passed.

## Earlier calibration findings

- The final baseline smoke completed 240 two-rank iterations in about ten
  minutes and wrote checkpoints every 40 iterations.
- All completion rows were valid; minimum effective sample count remained
  approximately 64.
- Measured peak reserved memory was about 0.9--1.1 GiB per rank, far below the
  configured approximately 9.2-GiB safe limit.
- The largest cross-rank tree in the completed conservative smoke was 2,327
  nodes.  Median/90th/95th/99th maximum-rank tree sizes were 29/116/568/1,842.
- AMP passed small identity smokes but failed the exact old/current-policy gate
  on a larger tree.  FP32 restored maximum log-probability error below `1e-5`,
  so the first long run remains FP32.
- Four reuse steps at learning rate `3e-4` were unusable (`clip_frac` about
  0.875 and `ratio_max` about 2.23).  The launch uses one on-policy step at
  `1e-4`.

## Branching finding

Preflop branching above one is unsafe as a long-run default because topology
depends strongly on the learned continuation probability:

- beta 1.75 produced a 3,772-node rank-zero tree;
- beta 1.6 overflowed the 4,096-node buffer on rank one;
- beta 1.25 completed the baseline smoke, but the higher-entropy A/B reached
  3,919 nodes before iteration 120.

The completed hour smoke showed that beta exactly 1.0 was too conservative for
public-tree coverage.  The long-run preset therefore uses the separately gated
1.10 intermediate setting.  It retains the 4,096-node hard cap and must still
be monitored because a 600-iteration gate cannot characterize a seven-hour
extreme tail.

## Learning-signal findings

The baseline (`c_h=0.05`, KL reference interval 25) often fell to policy
entropy around 0.4--0.6.  A controlled A/B with `c_h=0.1` and reference interval
100 kept entropy around 0.65--0.85 over its first 100 iterations and maintained
an active reference KL penalty.

A 1,200-hand duplicate arena comparing the A/B and baseline iteration-80
checkpoints reported `-13.4 +/- 59.1 bb/100` for the A/B.  This is statistically
indistinguishable from zero: it supports no performance claim either way.  The
higher-entropy settings are selected as a conservative exploration default,
not as an empirically optimal policy.

## How to select longer-run hyperparameters

Do not select by final training loss.  Use a staged, common-random-number
tournament:

1. Lock structural settings first: preflop beta 1.0, 4,096-node cap, FP32,
   64 completions, one update, and the calibrated model.
2. Sweep learning rate over `3e-5`, `1e-4`, and `3e-4` with identical seeds and
   equal iteration budgets.  Reject any run with identity failure, nonfinite
   metrics, invalid posterior mass, or reference KL that grows without bound.
3. At the winning learning rate, sweep `c_v` over `0.001`, `0.002`, and `0.005`.
   Compare pre-clip gradient norms and value explained variance; raw value-loss
   magnitude alone is not a gradient-balance measurement.
4. Compare `c_h` in `{0.05, 0.1}` and KL-reference intervals in `{100, 250}`.
   Require entropy not to collapse while keeping reference KL controlled.
5. Evaluate checkpoints 25%, 50%, 75%, and 100% through duplicate-deal arenas
   against the same baseline.  Use at least 1,200 hands for screening and grow
   the arena until the confidence interval is narrow enough to separate the
   leaders.  Select the best supported checkpoint, not necessarily the last.
6. Repeat the top setting with a second training seed before committing the
   remaining overnight budget.

Useful operational gates are: cross-rank maximum nodes below 80% of capacity,
zero invalid completion mass, finite losses, bounded reference KL, and no
systematic gradient clip scale below roughly 0.01.  Arena confidence intervals,
not those gates, decide strategic performance.
